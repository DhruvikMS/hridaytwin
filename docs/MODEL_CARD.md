# Model card: HridayTwin risk engine and twin

## Intended use
- **Users:** care teams (physicians, diabetes educators, nurse care-coordinators) running remote follow-up for adults with type 2 diabetes and/or hypertension.
- **Use:** prioritise outreach in the next 14 days, explain why, and compare candidate care plans before a teleconsultation.
- **Not intended for:** diagnosis, autonomous treatment changes, emergency triage, type 1 diabetes, pregnancy, under-18s, or any use without clinician review.

## Training data
Synthetic only: 1,200 patients x 180 days generated by `src/hridaytwin/synth.py`, with population priors from Indian studies (`docs/CLINICAL_RATIONALE.md`). Age 30 to 84; 55% male; five regions; 72% type 2 diabetes and 66% hypertension (every patient has at least one); 28% of people with diabetes have a CGM; 38% observe religious fasts.

## Outputs
- Probability of any adverse cardiometabolic event in the next 14 days, plus four event-type probabilities.
- Top drivers (SHAP) per prediction.
- Personal twin parameters with posterior sd.
- Simulated 90-day outcomes for candidate plans.

## Performance (synthetic, see `docs/RESULTS.md`)
| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| AUROC, composite, 5-fold patient-grouped CV | 0.726 (EHR-only 0.708; rules 0.615) |
| AUROC across 3 generator seeds | 0.727 ± 0.001 |
| Temporal + patient hold-out | 0.687 (EHR-only 0.681) |
| Severe hypoglycaemia / hyperglycaemic crisis / hypertensive emergency / acute CV | 0.943 / 0.858 / 0.888 / 0.711 |
| Calibration | Brier 0.042, ECE 0.005 |
| At 10 alerts per 100 patient-weeks | sensitivity 29%, PPV 13%, lift 2.9x, median lead time 7 days |

## Known limitations
1. **Synthetic training data.** Real performance is unknown until the validation plan is carried out.
2. Modest within-patient timing (0.56): it is better at *who* than *when*.
3. Acute CV events are driven mostly by baseline risk; the stream does not improve them here.
4. The twin does not learn individual activity sensitivity; activity what-ifs rely on literature priors.
5. New-drug effects in the simulator are population averages scaled by a shrunk personal response; they are not causal estimates for the individual.
6. Not tested for subgroup fairness on real data. The synthetic cohort lets us check performance by sex, region and age, but that only reflects the generator.

## Fairness and subgroup monitoring (deployment requirement)
Report AUROC, calibration and alert rate by sex, age band, region, rural/urban, CGM vs glucometer users and language of the app. Smartphone and CGM access correlate with income, so we also monitor missing-data rates: a patient who sends fewer readings must not quietly receive lower risk. In this PoC, missingness is an explicit feature (`wear_frac7`, `bp_n7`, `glucose_n7`), so the model can learn that silence is itself a signal.

## Ethical considerations
Alert fatigue, over-reliance and automation bias are the main risks. Mitigations: a budgeted alert rate, explanations on every alert, "for clinician review" on every plan, and no automatic actions. See `docs/ETHICS_PRIVACY.md`.
